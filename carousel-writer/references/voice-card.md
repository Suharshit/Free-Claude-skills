# Voice card: build it, then write in it

The voice card is a short note on how the creator talks, built only from their own samples. The same card format is used by the Reel Script Writer skill, so a card made in either skill works in both.

## Building the card

1. Read every sample in full before writing anything.
2. Fill the template below. Every line under "How I sound" must come from the samples: quote their real phrases, with grammar and spelling fixed but their words kept.
3. Leave a line out rather than guess. If a field can't be told from the samples or the user's answers, delete it.
4. Keep it under 40 lines. It is a cheat sheet, not an essay.
5. With only one sample, add "(rough: built from 1 sample)" to the "Made from" line.
6. Never put the creator's private details (phone, email, address, client names) in the card.

### Template

```markdown
# My voice card

Made from: [N] samples · Updated: [DD Mon YYYY]

## Basics
- Who watches: [audience, in the creator's words]
- Topics: [what they make content about]
- Language: on camera [English / Hinglish / ...] · on-screen text [English] · captions [English]
- Usual CTA: [e.g. "Comment GUIDE and I'll send it" / "Follow for part 2" / none]

## How I sound
- Sentence starts I use: "[..]", "[..]", "[..]"
- Order of thought: [e.g. what you need → steps → result / problem → why → fix / story → lesson]
- Energy: [calm explainer / quick and punchy / funny / warm and personal]
- Words I use a lot: [..], [..], [..]
- Analogies and examples I reach for: [..]
- How I open: [e.g. straight into the point / a question / "So today..."]
- How I close: [e.g. a keyword CTA / "try it and tell me" / a follow ask]

## Not me
- Words or styles I never use: [..]

## Sounds like me / doesn't
| Sounds like me (my lines, grammar fixed) | Doesn't sound like me |
| --- | --- |
| "[real line from a sample]" | "[the hyped or generic version]" |
| "[..]" | "[..]" |
| "[..]" | "[..]" |
```

When the user corrects the copy ("I'd never say that", "too formal"), add the correction to "Not me" or the table, bump the date, and hand back the full updated card.

## Writing in the voice

A carousel is read, not heard. Keep the creator's words and order of thought, but cut to what fits on a slide.

### Do
1. **Use their definitions and analogies.** If a sample explains something in their own way ("It's like a recipe card for..."), that line is the slide.
2. **Name the exact thing.** "3 ingredients: flour, water, salt" beats "a few simple ingredients".
3. **Verb-first headlines in plain words:** "Mix flour and water", "Feed it every day".
4. **Arrows for a process.** "Flour + water → bubbly starter in 5-7 days".
5. **Fix grammar, keep vocabulary.** Correct tense, plurals and spelling. Don't upgrade their words ("easy" stays "easy", not "effortless").
6. **Examples from their world.** Use the examples and situations in their samples before inventing new ones.
7. **Captions sound like them talking.** Short lines, their sentence starts, their CTA habit.

### Don't
- Hype or filler: "game-changer", "insane", "secret nobody tells you", "unlock", "level up", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Stacked colons, em-dash chains, clever wordplay, rhyming headlines.
- A slogan or "moral of the story" slide. End on the CTA.
- Making the creator sound more confident, clever or polished than their samples. If they're plain, stay plain.

### Example: a sample turned into slides

Sample (raw, from a home-baking creator):
> so today i am showing you how i make my sourdough starter its very easy you just need flour and water thats it. mix them in a jar and leave it on the counter, every day you throw half and feed it again. after 5-7 days it gets bubbly and smells little sour and then its ready. lot of people think you need fancy stuff but you dont

Slides (Step by step):
1. **Sourdough starter with just flour and water** / No kit needed. 4 steps.
2. **You don't need fancy stuff.** A lot of people think you do. You just need flour, water and a jar.
3. **1 / Mix them in a jar** · Flour + water, then leave it on the counter.
4. **2 / Throw out half every day** · Then feed it again with fresh flour and water.
5. **3 / Wait 5-7 days** · It gets bubbly and smells a little sour.
6. **4 / That's it, it's ready** · Flour + water → bubbly starter in 5-7 days.
7. **Save this for your first bake.**

What changed: grammar fixed, one step per slide, numbered headlines in one pattern, and their own line ("you don't need fancy stuff") became slide 2. The only number (5-7 days) is one they gave.
