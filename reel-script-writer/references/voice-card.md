# Voice card: build it, then write in it

The voice card is a short note on how the creator talks, built only from their own samples. The same card format is used by the Carousel Writer skill, so a card made in either skill works in both.

## Building the card

1. Read every sample in full before writing anything.
2. Fill the template below. Every line under "How I sound" must come from the samples: quote their real phrases, with grammar and spelling fixed but their words kept.
3. Leave a line out rather than guess. If a field can't be told from the samples or the user's answers, delete it.
4. Keep it under 40 lines. It is a cheat sheet, not an essay.
5. With only one sample, add "(rough: built from 1 sample)" to the "Made from" line.
6. Never put the creator's private details (phone, email, address, client names) in the card.

### Template

```markdown
# My voice card

Made from: [N] samples · Updated: [DD Mon YYYY]

## Basics
- Who watches: [audience, in the creator's words]
- Topics: [what they make content about]
- Language: on camera [English / Hinglish / ...] · on-screen text [English] · captions [English]
- Usual CTA: [e.g. "Comment GUIDE and I'll send it" / "Follow for part 2" / none]

## How I sound
- Sentence starts I use: "[..]", "[..]", "[..]"
- Order of thought: [e.g. what you need → steps → result / problem → why → fix / story → lesson]
- Energy: [calm explainer / quick and punchy / funny / warm and personal]
- Words I use a lot: [..], [..], [..]
- Analogies and examples I reach for: [..]
- How I open: [e.g. straight into the point / a question / "So today..."]
- How I close: [e.g. a keyword CTA / "try it and tell me" / a follow ask]

## Not me
- Words or styles I never use: [..]

## Sounds like me / doesn't
| Sounds like me (my lines, grammar fixed) | Doesn't sound like me |
| --- | --- |
| "[real line from a sample]" | "[the hyped or generic version]" |
| "[..]" | "[..]" |
| "[..]" | "[..]" |
```

When the user corrects a script ("I'd never say that", "too formal"), add the correction to "Not me" or the table, bump the date, and hand back the full updated card.

## Writing in the voice

The samples, and the card built from them, are the source. These moves turn them into a Reel without losing the person.

### Do
1. **Borrow their sentence starts.** Reuse the openers on the card. They are the creator's fingerprints.
2. **Keep their order of thought.** If they explain what you need → steps → result, so does the script.
3. **Name the exact thing.** If they would say "3 ingredients: flour, water, salt", never write "a few simple ingredients".
4. **Fix grammar, keep vocabulary.** Correct tense, plurals and spelling. Don't upgrade their words ("easy" stays "easy", not "effortless").
5. **One thought per line.** Split long "and... because..." sentences. Each line should be sayable in one breath.
6. **Add the hook, keep the rest.** Their natural first line is often warm-up ("So today I'm showing you..."). Replace only that with the hook, then carry on in their voice.
7. **Use their analogies** before inventing new ones.

### Don't
- Hype or filler: "game-changer", "insane", "secret nobody tells you", "let's dive in", "here's the thing", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Rhetorical triplets ("Faster. Smarter. Better.") and slogans.
- Ending on a moral. End on the CTA.
- Making the creator sound more confident, clever or polished than their samples. If they're plain, stay plain.

### Example: a sample turned into a Reel

Sample (raw, from a home-baking creator):
> so today i am showing you how i make my sourdough starter its very easy you just need flour and water thats it. mix them in a jar and leave it on the counter, every day you throw half and feed it again. after 5-7 days it gets bubbly and smells little sour and then its ready. lot of people think you need fancy stuff but you dont

Voice-over (Myth hook, Show how structure):
> You don't need a kit to make sourdough starter.
> You just need flour and water. That's it.
> Mix them in a jar and leave it on the counter.
> Every day, throw out half and feed it again.
> After 5 to 7 days, it gets bubbly and smells a little sour.
> Then it's ready.
> Save this for your first bake.

What changed: the warm-up line was swapped for a hook built from their own last sentence ("you don't need fancy stuff"), grammar was fixed, and long sentences were split. Every other phrase is the creator's, and the only number (5-7 days) is one they gave.
